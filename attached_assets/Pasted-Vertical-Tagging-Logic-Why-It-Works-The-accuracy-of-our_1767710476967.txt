Vertical Tagging Logic: Why It Works
The accuracy of our tagging comes from three key design decisions: rich capability profiles, explicit AI instructions, and low temperature settings. Here's exactly how it works.

The Core Principle: Comparative Profile Matching
The AI doesn't just tag based on keywords—it compares the article content against complete profiles of each business unit, then determines which profile has the strongest alignment. This is fundamentally different from simple keyword matching.

Each business unit profile contains four elements:

Companies: The actual subsidiary/division names
Summary: A narrative description of what the unit does
Keywords: Technical terms and concepts associated with that unit
Focus Areas: Types of contracts or work the unit pursues
The AI receives all three profiles simultaneously and must choose which one the content most closely resembles.

The AI Prompt Structure
The prompt explicitly asks the AI to:

"Analyze the following article and determine: Which business vertical it's most relevant to (defense, industrials, or advanced_programs)"

Then provides all three profiles side by side:

DEFENSE (Companies: TAS, IOMAX, USI):
- Summary: [narrative]
- Keywords: [list]
- Focus Areas: [list]
INDUSTRIALS (Companies: GCS, ICC, AMF):
- Summary: [narrative]
- Keywords: [list]
- Focus Areas: [list]
ADVANCED PROGRAMS (Companies: DRA, Onepath):
- Summary: [narrative]
- Keywords: [list]
- Focus Areas: [list]
By presenting all three options simultaneously with equal detail, the AI performs a comparative analysis rather than a threshold check.

Why This Works Better Than Keyword Matching
Simple keyword matching fails because:

"Aircraft maintenance" could be Defense (operational MRO) or Industrials (manufacturing facility)
"Sensors" could be Defense (ISR platforms) or Advanced Programs (R&D)
"UAS" could be Defense (operational drones) or Advanced Programs (autonomous systems research)
The AI resolves ambiguity by looking at context:

Article Content	Tagged As	Why
"Army awards $50M contract for MQ-9 sustainment"	Defense	Sustainment = operational support, MQ-9 = fielded platform
"AFRL seeks proposals for autonomous swarm AI"	Advanced Programs	AFRL + proposals + AI + emerging tech = R&D
"Boeing orders precision machined components"	Industrials	Manufacturing + supply chain focus
"DARPA tests new EW pod on F-16"	Advanced Programs	DARPA + testing + emerging tech
"F-16 depot maintenance contract awarded"	Defense	Depot maintenance = operational support
"New shipping container certified for weapons transport"	Industrials	Container + certification = manufacturing
The Temperature Setting
We use temperature: 0.3 which makes the AI more deterministic and consistent. Higher temperatures introduce creativity and variability—useful for writing, but bad for classification tasks.

Low temperature means:

Similar articles get tagged the same way every time
Less "creative interpretation"
More predictable, auditable results
How to Apply This to Task Order Tagging
For your task order matching system, structure your capability profiles the same way:

CAPABILITY AREA 1: [Name]
- Past Performance Contracts: [List actual contract names/numbers]
- Technical Keywords: [Specific terms from your PWS/SOW history]
- NAICS Codes: [Primary codes for this capability]
- Agencies: [Customers you've served]
- Summary: [What you actually do in this area]
CAPABILITY AREA 2: [Name]
- Past Performance Contracts: [...]
- Technical Keywords: [...]
...
Key principles to replicate:

Make profiles mutually exclusive where possible. The clearer the boundaries between categories, the more accurate the tagging.

Use actual contract names and technical terms from your past performance, not generic descriptions. "NAVAIR N00019-18-C-0001 F/A-18 depot maintenance" is better than "aircraft maintenance."

Include agency names in each profile. If you've done Army logistics and Navy logistics, those might be different capability areas.

Present all options simultaneously. The AI should choose the best fit, not decide if something meets a threshold.

Keep temperature low (0.2-0.4). Classification tasks need consistency, not creativity.

Require a single choice. Force the AI to pick ONE vertical, not multiple. Ambiguous content gets assigned to the strongest match.

The accuracy comes from specificity in the profiles and forcing a comparative choice. Generic descriptions produce generic (often wrong) results. Real contract names, real keywords from your actual work, and real agency relationships produce accurate tagging.
